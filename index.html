A family kitchen on King St

Ama Ko Momo started the way most good momo shops do — with a family recipe, a steamer, and a small storefront. The dough is mixed and rested daily; the fillings are seasoned by hand, never pre-mixed in bulk.

You’ll find both Nepali-style jhol momo (bathed in a light, spiced soup) and pan-fried Tibetan-style dumplings on the same menu — alongside sel roti, chowmein, and a short list of home-style sides.

  • Pleated by handEach momo is closed with roughly 16–18 folds — too fine for a machine, so every batch is shaped to order in the back kitchen.
  • Steamed in bambooTraditional stacked baskets keep the wrapper thin and translucent instead of gummy — the texture regulars come back for.
  • Achar made in-houseThe tomato-sesame chutney served alongside is blended fresh, with the chili heat adjusted table by table on request.
